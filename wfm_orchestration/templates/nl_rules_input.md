A creature either holds a condition or it does not; there is no intermediate state. When multiple effects impose the same condition on a creature, each instance carries its own duration, but the effects of that condition do not increase beyond what a single instance produces. A condition ends when it is countered by a specified action or when its duration expires.
A blinded creature cannot see. A blinded creature fails any ability check that requires sight. Attack rolls made against a blinded creature have advantage. Attack rolls made by a blinded creature have disadvantage.
A charmed creature cannot make attack rolls targeting the charmer. A charmed creature cannot target the charmer with harmful abilities. A charmed creature cannot target the charmer with harmful magical effects. Any ability check the charmer makes to interact socially with the charmed creature has advantage.
A deafened creature cannot hear. A deafened creature fails any ability check that requires hearing.
A creature's exhaustion level is an integer value between 0 and 6. A creature with an exhaustion level of 0 is not exhausted. When an effect imposes exhaustion on a creature, the creature's exhaustion level increases by the amount specified by that effect. A creature's exhaustion level cannot exceed 6. A creature with an exhaustion level of 6 dies. A creature with an exhaustion level of 5 or higher has a speed of 0. A creature with an exhaustion level of 4 or higher has a hit point maximum equal to half its normal hit point maximum, rounded down. A creature with an exhaustion level of 3 or higher has disadvantage on attack rolls. A creature with an exhaustion level of 3 or higher has disadvantage on saving throws. A creature with an exhaustion level of 2 or higher has a speed equal to half its normal speed, rounded down. A creature with an exhaustion level of 1 or higher has disadvantage on ability checks. When an effect removes exhaustion from a creature, the creature's exhaustion level decreases by the amount specified by that effect. If a creature's exhaustion level would fall below 1 as a result of removal, it is set to 0. A creature that finishes a long rest and has consumed both food and drink during that rest has its exhaustion level reduced by 1.
A frightened creature has disadvantage on ability checks while the source of its fear is within its line of sight. A frightened creature has disadvantage on attack rolls while the source of its fear is within its line of sight. A frightened creature cannot willingly move to any position that is closer to the source of its fear than its current position.
A grappled creature has a speed of 0. A grappled creature cannot gain any bonus to its speed. A grapple condition on a creature ends if the grappler becomes unable to take actions or reactions. A grapple condition on a creature ends if an effect moves the grappled creature to a position outside the reach of the grappler.
A creature that cannot take actions or reactions is incapacitated.
An invisible creature cannot be seen by any means except magic or a special sense. For the purpose of determining whether an invisible creature can hide, it is treated as heavily obscured. The location of an invisible creature can be detected through sounds it produces or tracks it leaves. Attack rolls made against an invisible creature have disadvantage. Attack rolls made by an invisible creature have advantage.
A paralyzed creature cannot take actions or reactions. A paralyzed creature cannot move. A paralyzed creature cannot speak. A paralyzed creature fails any Strength saving throw. A paralyzed creature fails any Dexterity saving throw. Attack rolls made against a paralyzed creature have advantage. Any attack roll that hits a paralyzed creature is a critical hit if the attacker's distance to the paralyzed creature is 5 feet or less.
A petrified creature is transformed into a solid inanimate substance. Any nonmagical object the petrified creature is wearing is also transformed into that substance. Any nonmagical object the petrified creature is carrying is also transformed into that substance. The petrified creature's weight becomes ten times its normal weight. The petrified creature ceases aging. A petrified creature cannot take actions or reactions. A petrified creature cannot move. A petrified creature cannot speak. A petrified creature is unaware of its surroundings. Attack rolls made against a petrified creature have advantage. A petrified creature fails any Strength saving throw. A petrified creature fails any Dexterity saving throw. A petrified creature has resistance to all damage types. A petrified creature is immune to the poisoned condition. A petrified creature is immune to disease. Any poison already present in the petrified creature's system at the time of petrification is suspended rather than neutralized; it does not progress and does not end.
A poisoned creature has disadvantage on attack rolls. A poisoned creature has disadvantage on ability checks.
A prone creature's only form of movement is crawling, unless it takes the action to stand up. When a prone creature stands up, the prone condition ends. A prone creature has disadvantage on attack rolls. An attack roll against a prone creature has advantage if the attacker's distance to the prone creature is 5 feet or less. An attack roll against a prone creature has disadvantage if the attacker's distance to the prone creature is greater than 5 feet.
A restrained creature has a speed of 0. A restrained creature cannot gain any bonus to its speed. Attack rolls made against a restrained creature have advantage. Attack rolls made by a restrained creature have disadvantage. A restrained creature has disadvantage on Dexterity saving throws.
A stunned creature cannot take actions or reactions. A stunned creature cannot move. A stunned creature can only speak falteringly; it cannot speak in a deliberate or coherent manner. A stunned creature fails any Strength saving throw. A stunned creature fails any Dexterity saving throw. Attack rolls made against a stunned creature have advantage.
An unconscious creature cannot take actions or reactions. An unconscious creature cannot move. An unconscious creature cannot speak. An unconscious creature is unaware of its surroundings. An unconscious creature releases any object it is holding at the moment it becomes unconscious. An unconscious creature falls prone at the moment it becomes unconscious. An unconscious creature fails any Strength saving throw. An unconscious creature fails any Dexterity saving throw. Attack rolls made against an unconscious creature have advantage. Any attack roll that hits an unconscious creature is a critical hit if the attacker's distance to the unconscious creature is 5 feet or less.
